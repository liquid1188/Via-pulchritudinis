From Sacred Architecture to Personal Style

Every look begins with a place. The architecture of a church, its materials, colors, and atmosphere, becomes the starting point for a contemporary wardrobe.

These churches are sacred places before they are sources of inspiration. They were built for prayer and worship, and the looks here are a small tribute to their beauty, not a substitute for visiting them.

  1. 01

    The Architecture

    Each look begins with a church. Its exterior and interior reveal a distinctive harmony of stone, stained glass, wood, metal, and ornament.

  2. 02

    The Palette

    Five colors are drawn from the building’s architectural character, capturing something of its atmosphere and visual identity.

  3. 03

    The Wardrobe

    Those colors become the inspiration for a contemporary outfit, bringing the beauty of sacred architecture into the art of everyday dress.

A Palette, Not a Formula

Each palette is measured from an exterior and an interior photograph, then checked by eye so it reflects the building’s real stone, glass, wood, and metal rather than the accident of a cloudy day or a camera’s color cast.

The outfit is chosen first for its own beauty and good taste. A color-match score in each look shows how closely the clothes follow the palette, but it is a guide, not a verdict.

VIA PULCHRITUDINISA Catholic Journal of Style and Beauty

Via Pulchritudinis explores the relationship between sacred architecture, personal style, and the Catholic understanding of beauty. Through its architectural lookbook, theological reflections, and curated resources, the journal invites readers to discover new ways of appreciating the Church’s artistic inheritance.

Curated by Andrew Likoudis, a Catholic writer and editor whose work on St. Pier Giorgio Frassati and personal style appeared in the National Catholic Register.
